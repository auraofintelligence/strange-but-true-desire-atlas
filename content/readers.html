Australian and Canadian evidence released in 2025–2026

Start with real readers, buyers and genre neighbours.

No sound 2025–2026 primary study located in this research pass isolates science-fiction romance as its own worldwide audience. The strongest public evidence measures adjacent categories and different behaviours: Australian reading, genre preferences and discovery; Canadian readers and buyers; and smaller romance-community snapshots. Reader, buyer, purchase and sales figures remain separate.

Signals for this catalogue

The useful picture appears at the intersections.

These findings do not collapse into one imaginary global reader. They show where Luke's catalogue can listen: the junction of romance, fantasy and science fiction; changing format habits; trusted discovery networks; series reading; audio; and reader interest in how AI participates in books.

Romance, fantasy and science fiction travel across formats

Among English-speaking Canadian fiction readers in each format, romance reached 29% in print, 36% in ebook and 30% in audio. Fantasy reached 30%, 31% and 33%; science fiction reached 33%, 28% and 34% respectively.

Each format has a separate fiction-reader base. These are reader shares, not market shares. Open the BookNet study ↗

Australian discovery is social and physical

Creative Australia's general-population sample reported word of mouth at 39%, libraries 29%, bookshops 27%, social media 22%, online retailers 20%, reviews 19% and forums including Reddit 10%. Respondents could name several paths.

Canadian format preference still favours print

Among surveyed English-speaking Canadian readers aged 18+, 58% preferred print, 18% ebook, 16% audiobook and 8% had no preference. Annual use was broader and overlapping: 93% print, 71% ebook and 57% audio.

One fantasy-romance forum reveals its desired blend

In a 2026 self-selected r/FantasyRomance census, 44.2% wanted equal fantasy and romance and 39.9% wanted more fantasy (n=1,019 for that question). Ebook use reached 83.6% (n=1,020). This is a participating community snapshot, not a population survey.

Australian romance communities add a different pulse

In the Australian Romance Readers Association's self-selected 2025 survey of 216 people, 51.92% read fantasy romance; 84.73% used ebooks, 77.34% physical books and 37.44% audio. The group is useful for community listening, not national inference.

Canadian buyers notice AI involvement

Across all 1,979 surveyed English-speaking Canadian adults, 65% answered yes or sometimes when asked whether they wanted to know if AI helped produce any aspect of a book. Among the 976 buyers, that figure was 69%; only 3% of buyers first became aware of the purchased book through an AI tool.

Audio and series signals

Several doors can lead into the same future world.

An Australian National University study released in June 2026 surveyed 503 Australians who had listened to at least one audiobook in the previous year: 59.2% listened weekly or more and 56.3% listened to popular-genre fiction. It measures audiobook listeners, not national prevalence.

BookNet found 37% of surveyed English-speaking Canadian readers read books in a series in 2025. Together these signals support testing series continuity and audio production without assuming either format will work automatically.
