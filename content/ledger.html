Five visible lanes

Different truths can share a table without sharing authority.

The labels below are the central design rule of the Atlas. Each card, claim and visual should make it possible to tell what kind of thing you are looking at and who still needs to review it.

Source-grounded

Evidence with a trail

A named source supports the narrow claim. The year, country, format, sample or legal jurisdiction stays attached.

Proposed pathway

A direction to test

A plan, goal or design choice that might work. It carries assumptions, a small next step and a condition that could change the decision.

Source-inspired fiction

Reality as a launchpad

A story grows from a law, trend, place or research question, but its characters, systems and events are invented.

Fictional wild card

Possibility with no borrowed proof

A deliberately bold invention. It may be philosophically useful without being feasible, legal or scientifically demonstrated.

Review gate

A human decision remains

Legal, cultural, ethical, editorial, accessibility or community authority is still needed before action or release.

Worked ledger

The label changes the sentence.

The same topic can produce several honest statements. The problem begins when a hope quietly borrows the authority of a statistic or a fictional institution is drawn like current law.
