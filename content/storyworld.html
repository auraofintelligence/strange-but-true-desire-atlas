Speculative proposition

What if family became more generous without becoming less free?

Global group marriage appears here as fiction, philosophy and social-design provocation. It is not a claim of present legal recognition, a recruitment plan or guidance for avoiding marriage, immigration, tax, inheritance or family law.

Real relationship conduct, legal marriage, ceremonies, parentage, property, tax and migration are separate questions in every jurisdiction.

“Not a replacement of marriage, but its metamorphosis.”

Luke Nathan Hayes · speculative philosophy

Storyworld invariants

Nobody is absorbed into the system.

A plural future earns its hope by protecting the least powerful person in the room. Every adult keeps identity, privacy, outside relationships, independent advice and a practical path to leave.

Autonomy

Shared life, separate personhood

Private rooms, independent devices, direct access to money and personal friendships remain normal rather than suspicious.

Consent

Every yes has a subject and a time

Joining a household does not create blanket consent. Affection, intimacy, care work, business and publicity are negotiated separately.

Exit

A door that works in practice

Leaving is possible without losing identity papers, housing access, personal records, community standing or a fair share of agreed property.

Joyful Responsible Abundance

All three words have work to do.

Joy creates room for play, beauty and chosen connection. Responsibility keeps consent, law, care, resources and consequences visible. Abundance rejects the idea that love must be zero-sum, while admitting that time, housing, energy and attention are still finite.

Joyful

Desire and delight can matter without becoming entitlement. Characters retain humour, wonder and the right to change their minds.

Responsible

Power, care work, money, law, culture, health, children and genuine exit are not background decoration.

Abundance

More connection is possible, but the story must show how resources, boundaries and attention are shared fairly.

Present law

The fiction label stays on.

The Australian Marriage Act 1961 and Canadian Criminal Code section 293 are two examples of why a simple “legal group marriage map” would mislead. A story may imagine new institutions. A real edition must describe current law accurately.
