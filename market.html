Momentum is real. Certainty is not.

Tracked US print romance, UK publisher revenue and a Canadian title subset all show strong movement. They do not add up to a global romance market size, and they say little about one new author's likely sales.

What the numbers can do

Use a signal to form a question.

A signal can justify a cover test, reader interview, metadata experiment or audiobook quote. It cannot prove that a trope, heat level or rapid-release plan will work for a specific story.

Observed

Romance print moved in the US

Ask which subgenres, formats and retail environments drove the tracked movement, and what was not captured.

Observed

UK fiction and audio grew

Ask whether the work and budget suit audio, and how export rights or production costs alter the route.

Test next

Reader language beats category fog

Test whether readers call the work romance, romantasy, science-fiction romance or speculative relationship fiction.

Format horizon

One story can travel through several doors.

Print, ebook, subscription reading and audio have different economics and discovery systems. Build the story contract first, then choose the format sequence that can be produced and supported well.

Print

Useful for events, gifts and readers who want an object. Unit economics depend on trim, page count, colour, returns and shipping.

Ebook

Fast to update and distribute. Exclusivity, price rules, mature-content metadata and territory controls can change the route.

Audio

Powerful for performance-led romance, but narration, editing, rights, casting and quality control require a real budget.

Evidence gap

Current comparable romance unit sales, author earnings and reader-gender data for Australia, India, Japan and the UAE remain unverified. The Atlas will not fill those gaps with global estimates from commercial market-report summaries.
